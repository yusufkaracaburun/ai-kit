# Existing-design review

Review the system, not isolated taste. Inspect representative desktop and
mobile screens plus key states before proposing a refresh.

## Audit lenses

Score 1–5 and cite visible evidence for:

- audience recognition and value clarity above the fold;
- hierarchy, navigation, CTA prominence, trust, and conversion friction;
- brand distinctiveness versus current competitors;
- palette roles, typography, spacing, grid, imagery, iconography, and motion;
- consistency across components and states;
- responsive behavior at narrow and extra-wide widths;
- contrast, focus, target size, reduced motion, and color-independent states;
- performance risks caused by fonts, imagery, effects, or animation.

## Output

Start with a compact diagnosis, then use:

| Element or pattern | Preserve | Change | Remove | Evidence and reason |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Separate defects from preferences. Rank findings by user/business impact, not
visual annoyance. Identify the smallest token-level changes that solve repeated
issues.

For refresh mode, copy the same representative page into three separately named
variants and keep the original untouched. Preserve content, hierarchy, and task
so visual directions remain comparable. Recommend a direction only after the
scorecard and do not merge it into the existing design until the user selects it.
